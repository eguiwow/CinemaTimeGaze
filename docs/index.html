Cinema’s Temporal Gaze

Every film is made in one year and set in another — and most of the time those are the same year. Pick a decade and watch where its films look: back below the line, forward above it, and the great still mass of films set in their own present collapsed into the grey bar at the centre.

What each decade did with its time

A median tells you the middle film and nothing about the spread, so here is the spread. One bar per release decade, counting only the films that actually travel: everything set in its own present is pulled out and reported on the right, because at 60 to 80% of every decade it would flatten everything else into a sliver. Reading left to right you are walking from the deep past, through the near past, across the line, and out into the future; the darker the shade, the further that slice goes. Decades with fewer than ten films to count are left out rather than guessed at.

Every film at once

The year a film came out along the bottom, the year it depicts up the side, one dot for every year any film points at. The diagonal is cinema set in its own moment, and almost everything sits on it. Below that line is memory; above it is speculation — and you can see at a glance how much thinner the air gets up there. The decade you picked above is drawn solid, the rest faint behind it. This is also the sanity check: if this and the arcs disagree, the aggregation is wrong.

How this was built, and what is wrong with it
  • Corpus. 3860 films from TMDB, 1900–2026, sampled per release year and ranked within the year by TMDB vote count, covering 37 national cinemas across 10 regions. It is not evenly spread: US alone is 58% of it, so any figure you read without picking an origin is mostly a statement about US. Vote count is a popularity measure, and popularity travels badly between film industries — a well-known film from a small country can sit below an obscure one from a large one.
  • Labels are model judgments, not extractions. 55% of films were classified from world knowledge rather than from a stated year in the source text, because the source text is a ~90-word Wikipedia lead that usually does not name the setting. Those labels have not been checked against an independent ground truth. Treat the shape as a hypothesis, not a measurement.
  • Present is the default and the default is sticky. A film whose setting is never stated and that the model does not recognise lands on "present". That biases the whole picture toward the diagonal, so real nostalgia is probably understated.
  • Medians, never means. One film set in the year 802,701 outweighs a thousand set last Tuesday. Every figure here is a median; the means are meaningless and are not shown.
  • The year axis is piecewise. The dense 1850–2050 band gets most of the width; deep past and far future are compressed, with dashed breaks marking the joins. Distances across a break are not comparable.
  • Re-releases contaminate. A 2013 3-D re-release of a 1993 film reads as a 20-year lookback. Rare, but present.
