Cinema’s Temporal Gaze

Every film is made in one year and set in another — and most of the time those are the same year. Pick a decade and watch where its films look: back below the line, forward above it, and the great still mass of films set in their own present collapsed into the grey bar at the centre.

What each decade did with its time

A median tells you the middle film and nothing about the spread, so here is the spread. One bar per release decade, counting only the films that actually travel: everything set in its own present is pulled out and reported on the right, because at 60 to 80% of every decade it would flatten everything else into a sliver. Reading left to right you are walking from the deep past, through the near past, across the line, and out into the future; the darker the shade, the further that slice goes. Decades with fewer than ten films to count are left out rather than guessed at.

Every film, every year. All at once.

The year a film came out along the bottom, the year it depicts up the side, one dot for every year any film points at. The diagonal is cinema set in its own moment, and almost everything sits on it. Below that line is memory; above it is speculation — and you can see at a glance how much thinner the air gets up there. The decade you picked above is drawn solid, the rest faint behind it. This is also the sanity check: if this and the arcs disagree, the aggregation is wrong.

Methodology — how this was built, and where it breaks

Three steps. Each one has a failure mode, and the third exists to measure the second.

  1. Take a sample of films. __SOURCE_NOTE__
  2. Ask a language model what year each film depicts. Every film goes to the model with its title, release year, genres and a short published summary, and comes back with the year or years it is set in, how much of the film sits in each, and — the part that matters — whether that came from a year stated in the text or from the model's own knowledge of the film. Those summaries run to about ninety words and usually do not name a setting, so __KPCT__% of the labels here rest on world knowledge rather than on anything written down. Where the model knows the film, that is a strength. Where it does not, it is a guess wearing the clothes of a reading, and there is no way to tell the two apart from the outside.
  3. Score those labels against a second, independent pass. __ACCURACY_CAVEAT__

What the method leaves wrong, roughly in the order it matters:

  • Present is the default, and the default is sticky. A film whose setting is never stated and that the model does not recognise lands on "present". That pulls the whole picture toward the diagonal, so if anything is being missed here it is nostalgia, not invented.
  • A popular sample is not cinema. Films are ranked within each release year by vote count, which measures how many people on one website have got round to rating something. It travels badly between film industries, and it thins out sharply the further back you go.
  • Medians, never means. One film set in the year 802,701 outweighs a thousand set last Tuesday. Every figure on this page is a median; the means are not shown because they would be meaningless.
  • The year axis is piecewise. The dense 1850–2050 band gets most of the width, deep past and far future are compressed, and dashed breaks mark the joins. A distance measured across a break is not comparable to one measured inside it.
  • "Set in" is taken literally. A film about the 1970s made as an argument about now counts as looking back, because that is what the rule says, and a rule that bends for allegory stops being a rule.
  • Re-releases contaminate. A 2013 3-D re-release of a 1993 film reads as a twenty-year lookback. Rare, but present.
Where the idea came from

A dream. Not as a figure of speech — an actual night's sleep, in which cinema had a direction of gaze the way a compass has north, and some films pointed hard away from the year that made them. It seemed on waking like the kind of thing somebody would obviously have plotted already.

Nobody had, as far as I could find. So: a sample of films, a classifier to ask each one what year it depicts, and a chart to see whether the century spends more time remembering or predicting. The answer turned out to be neither — overwhelmingly, cinema is set in the year it was made, and the interesting part is the small fraction that travels.

The dream version was more confident than this one. It did not come with a note about how often the model is guessing.
